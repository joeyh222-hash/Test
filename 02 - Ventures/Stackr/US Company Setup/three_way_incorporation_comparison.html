The verdict — why Clerky Lifetime still wins for your situation
Stripe Atlas ($500) is fast, polished, and the $50k in partner perks is genuinely valuable — especially the $2,500 in Stripe credits. But it's primarily a formation tool. Once you're incorporated, it hands you templates rather than managed legal workflows. It has no stock option issuance, no unlimited hiring docs, and no ongoing corporate maintenance. Great if you plan to use Stripe as your payment processor, but for hardware sold on Kickstarter you'll likely use Kickstarter's own payment system — making the Stripe ecosystem lock-in less relevant.
Clerky Lifetime ($819) costs $319 more than Atlas but gives you unlimited SAFEs, unlimited hiring documents, unlimited NDAs, stock plan adoption, and unlimited corporate maintenance — all for life. For a hardware startup heading toward investor fundraising with a growing team, you'll use every one of those features. The legal doc quality is also specifically designed for VC-backed startups, which matters when investors do due diligence.
Clerky Pay Per Use ($427+) is the worst option for your stage — it looks cheap upfront but costs more than everything else once you factor in the per-action fees.
